\section{Cryptographic Inference: MPC and FHE}
\label{sec:crypto}

% Mechanism-section template (consistent across §3-§7):
%   Approach · Representative schemes (table) · Limitations / known attacks.
% Scope: INFERENCE only. The cryptographic RAG retrieval/storage layer is §8 (sec:retrieval).

Cryptographic schemes compute on data the server never sees in the clear: secure multi-party
computation (MPC) and secret sharing (SS) split the computation across non-colluding parties,
while fully-homomorphic encryption (FHE) evaluates directly on ciphertext. They offer the
strongest confidentiality in our taxonomy (\cref{sec:threat})---a trusted computing base of
\emph{none} (FHE) or \emph{non-colluding parties} (MPC/SS), no special hardware---at one-to-three
orders of magnitude in latency or communication, concentrated in the non-linear operations. Because
MPC/FHE \emph{inference} protocols are already the subject of dedicated surveys---Li et
al.~\citep{li_pti_survey} (layer-wise, MPC-vs-HE) and Chen et al.~\citep{chen_sok_pti} (a
reproducibility re-benchmark)---we do not re-systematize them, covering the sub-family briefly
through five frontier exemplars (\cref{tab:inf-perf}) that locate it on our deployment-cost frontier
and deferring the cryptographic \emph{retrieval} layer (PIR/ORAM/SSE/DCPE) to \cref{sec:retrieval}.
On our criteria, cryptographic inference scores highest on threat-model fit and fidelity but lowest
on performance (\cref{sec:synthesis}).

\subsection{Approach}
\label{ssec:crypto-approach}
\emph{Secret sharing and MPC} split each value into shares held by non-colluding parties; linear
maps---the bulk of a Transformer's matrix multiplications---are local or need only cheap
Beaver-triple multiplications, so cost concentrates in the non-linear operations (Softmax, GELU,
LayerNorm), which must be evaluated through oblivious transfer or garbled circuits or replaced by
polynomial approximations---each adding rounds, communication, or error. \emph{FHE} instead
evaluates directly on ciphertext under one key; the RNS-CKKS scheme used in most FHE Transformer
inference is \emph{leveled}, bounding multiplicative depth before an expensive bootstrap and again
forcing the non-linearities into low-degree polynomial approximations. In both families these
non-linear operations dominate---Li et al.~\citep{li_pti_survey} measure them at $>$85\% of MPC
runtime and ${\approx}60\%$ for HE---and autoregressive decoding compounds the cost, since each
generated token is a full forward pass whose per-pass overhead and interaction rounds multiply by
the generation length~\citep{andreoletti_ppi_practice}.

Our five exemplars (\cref{tab:inf-perf}) span the design space: 2PC (SHAFT), 2PC with an offline
dealer (PermLLM), distributed MPC with evaluator nodes (Fission), private mixture-of-experts
routing (CryptoMoE), and non-interactive FHE (Euston). CryptoMoE is the one architecture-specific
case: it keeps MoE expert-routing secret-shared, hiding a pattern that leaks the input's semantic
type and has been used to recover a co-batched prompt in full~\citep{yona_moe_steal}, recovering
sparsity at $2.8$--$3.5\times$ dense-MPC cost. Across all five, even the fastest exchanges hundreds
of MB to GBs per token or inference at exact (MPC) or near-exact (FHE) fidelity; no scheme reports
overhead vs.\ plaintext, so \cref{tab:inf-perf}'s figures are paper-reported or 1-hop extrapolated
(\cref{sec:comparison}).

\paragraph{The trust assumption is the security.}
Every scheme here is proved secure only against a \emph{semi-honest} adversary, and MPC/SS
additionally require non-collusion. Neither is free: Muse shows a single \emph{malicious} client can
extract the server's weights from a semi-honest 2PC protocol~\citep{muse_2021}, and two colluding
parties collapse additive sharing back to plaintext. Closing the malicious-client gap costs
${\approx}1.3$--$2\times$ (SIMC~\citep{simc_2022}) and full malicious security more---no scheme we
survey adopts either. So under a realistic cloud adversary that can deviate or quietly co-locate its
``non-colluding'' parties, the guarantee degrades to that of an honest operator---comparable in
trust to a TEE, but without the attestation that lets a client \emph{verify} it (\cref{sec:tee},
\cref{sec:hybrid}).

\paragraph{Permutation secrecy is heuristic, not information-theoretic.}
The fastest exemplars buy speed by evaluating non-linearities on \emph{permuted} plaintext rather
than under MPC (PermLLM reveals permuted hidden states; Fission reveals shuffled activation chunks
to evaluators), which both concede is not information-theoretic. Hidden No More reconstructs prompts
near-perfectly from \emph{fixed} permutations via decoder injectivity, breaking STIP, Centaur, and
PermLLM in a third-party-node deployment~\citep{hnm}; the co-designed token-sharding defence is only
\emph{statistically} secure~\citep{cascade_thomas}. Exposure is contingent on \emph{who} sees the
reveal---PermLLM reveals to the user (their own data), and Fission's chunking drives leakage down as
${\sim}\log N$---so permutation is best read as a heuristic speed/leakage trade, not a guarantee on
par with full MPC or FHE.

\paragraph{FHE: depth budget and approximate-ciphertext security.}
RNS-CKKS's bounded multiplicative depth forces range-sensitive polynomial approximations of the
non-linearities. Separately, approximate-FHE ciphertexts carry a subtlety: the
IND-CPA\textsuperscript{D} attack recovers a CKKS secret key when an adversary can \emph{observe
decryptions} of honestly evaluated ciphertexts~\citep{li_micciancio_2021}, mitigated by noise
flooding. It bites only where decrypted outputs are exposed; the non-interactive inference schemes
here (the server never decrypts) are unaffected, but RAG deployments that return decrypted scores to
a client should apply the countermeasure (\cref{ssec:retr-limits}).
