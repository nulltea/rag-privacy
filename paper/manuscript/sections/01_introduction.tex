\subsection{Scope}
% Transformer inference AND RAG, including graph-RAG (a setting Bodea excludes).
% Five mechanism families. Semi-honest baseline (range to malicious documented in §2).
%
% TWO SCOPE CORRECTIONS (decision of record):
%  (1) PRACTICALITY: we systematize state-of-the-art, *practical* schemes --- those
%      with good security AND low-enough overhead to deploy. Foundational/superseded
%      works are cited for lineage only, never in the comparison tables.
%  (2) COMMODITY HARDWARE: we prioritize schemes that need NO specialized confidential-
%      compute hardware (no NVIDIA H100/B200 confidential GPUs). Pure-TEE-on-CC-GPU is
%      the performance/trust BASELINE (\cref{sec:tee}); the protagonist is the HYBRID
%      SPLIT family (CPU-TEE + commodity GPU, \cref{sec:hybrid}).
We scope the systematization to \emph{confidential Transformer inference and RAG on
commodity hardware}, and impose two deliberate restrictions. First, \textbf{practical
frontier}: the comparison tables hold only state-of-the-art schemes that combine
credible security with deployable overhead; foundational or superseded work is cited
for lineage only. Second, \textbf{commodity hardware}: we prioritize schemes that need
no specialized confidential-compute accelerator (no NVIDIA H100/B200 confidential GPU).
Pure-TEE inference on a confidential GPU is kept as the performance and trust
\emph{baseline} (\cref{sec:tee}); the protagonist family is the \emph{hybrid split}
(CPU-TEE + an untrusted commodity GPU, \cref{sec:hybrid}). The systematization spans
Transformer inference (embedding, reranking, generation) and RAG including
\emph{graph-RAG}---a setting the prior RAG-privacy SoK~\citep{bodea_sok} excludes. We
treat a semi-honest cloud as the baseline adversary, documenting the range to malicious
in \cref{sec:threat}. Explicitly \emph{out of scope}: microarchitectural
side-channels (a field-wide exclusion we make a finding of, \cref{sec:threat}),
full verifiable computation (lightweight composable integrity is in scope, but
zkLLM-class proving is not), and foundational/superseded schemes.

\subsection{Contributions}
This paper is a \emph{systematization of knowledge}. Concretely:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A six-axis threat-model taxonomy} (\cref{sec:threat}) that
        classifies confidential Transformer-inference and RAG schemes by
        adversary intent, trust
        anchor, attacker knowledge, observation surface, side-channel scope,
        and protection goal --- and exposes a field-wide side-channel blind spot.
  \item \textbf{A mechanism-organized systematization} (\cref{sec:crypto}--%
        \cref{sec:hybrid}) of five defense families across the full
        inference-and-RAG pipeline, including graph-RAG as a retrieval setting.
  \item \textbf{A performance comparison} synthesized from reported numbers
        (\cref{sec:synthesis}) --- the cost axis the existing RAG-privacy SoK
        \cite{bodea_sok} omits --- with an explicit non-comparability caveat.
  \item \textbf{A stage\,$\times$\,mechanism gap-map} (\cref{sec:synthesis})
        whose empty cells are the field's open problems.
  \item \textbf{A focused vignette} (\cref{sec:obfuscation}): static
        obfuscation's reported resistance does not hold under the realistic
        threat model. We report the \emph{finding}; the attack methodology and a
        defense are deferred to a companion paper.
\end{itemize}
