\subsection{Scope}
% Transformer inference AND RAG, including graph-RAG (a setting Bodea excludes).
% Five mechanism families. Semi-honest baseline (range to malicious documented in §2).
%
% ORGANIZING GOAL = DEPLOYMENT READINESS (decision of record 2026-06-02): assess the
% whole field by likelihood of real-world adoption on 3 criteria -- (1) performance,
% (2) fidelity (output quality), (3) threat-model FIT (appropriate, not maximal). We do
% NOT champion one family; hybrid-split/obfuscation lead as a FINDING (good perf-to-threat
% ratio), established in §8, not assumed here. Hardware-trust TIERS (Tier1 none / Tier2
% CPU-TEE / Tier3 confidential accelerator) feed criteria 1+3; Tier3 = baseline, deferred
% to accel_tee_sok. PRACTICAL-FRONTIER restriction = a consequence of the lens.
Our organizing goal is \emph{deployment readiness}: we assess the field by how likely a
scheme is to be adopted in a real deployment, on three criteria---\textbf{performance}
(overhead and latency), \textbf{fidelity} (impact on output accuracy and quality), and
\textbf{threat-model fit} (an adversary model \emph{appropriate} to the deployment, not
maximal in a purely academic sense). The aim is not to crown a winning family but to
make this trade explicit; which schemes sit near the deployable frontier is a
\emph{finding} of the synthesis (\cref{sec:synthesis}), not an assumption.

Two structural choices follow. First, a \emph{hardware-trust} ladder, defined with the
trust-anchor axis of our taxonomy (\cref{sec:threat}), feeds the performance and
threat-fit criteria: Tier~1 (no trusted hardware), Tier~2 (CPU-TEE only, commodity GPU
untrusted), and Tier~3 (a confidential accelerator trusted end-to-end). We focus on
Tiers~1 and~2, where confidentiality is an open \emph{design} problem, and keep
\textbf{Tier~3} as a performance baseline (\cref{sec:tee}): once a confidential
accelerator is trusted end-to-end, confidentiality largely reduces to hardware
availability, enclave construction, and attestation---a narrower, hardware-specific
space systematized in its own right~\citep{accel_tee_sok}. Second, the
\textbf{practical-frontier} restriction follows directly from the deployment lens:
comparison tables hold only state-of-the-art schemes with deployable overhead, and
foundational or superseded work is cited for lineage only.

The systematization spans Transformer inference (embedding, reranking, generation) and
RAG including \emph{graph-RAG}---a setting the prior RAG-privacy SoK~\citep{bodea_sok}
excludes. We treat a semi-honest cloud as the baseline adversary, documenting the range
to malicious in \cref{sec:threat}. Explicitly \emph{out of scope}: microarchitectural
side-channels (a field-wide exclusion we make a finding of, \cref{sec:threat}), full
verifiable computation (lightweight composable integrity is in scope, but zkLLM-class
proving is not), and foundational/superseded schemes.

\subsection{Contributions}
This paper is a \emph{systematization of knowledge}. Concretely:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A six-axis threat-model taxonomy} (\cref{sec:threat}) that
        classifies confidential Transformer-inference and RAG schemes by
        adversary intent, trust
        anchor, attacker knowledge, observation surface, side-channel scope,
        and protection goal --- and exposes a field-wide side-channel blind spot.
  \item \textbf{A mechanism-organized systematization} (\cref{sec:crypto}--%
        \cref{sec:hybrid}) of five defense families across the full
        inference-and-RAG pipeline, including graph-RAG as a retrieval setting.
  \item \textbf{A performance comparison} synthesized from reported numbers
        (\cref{sec:synthesis}) --- the cost axis the existing RAG-privacy SoK
        \cite{bodea_sok} omits --- with an explicit non-comparability caveat.
  \item \textbf{A deployment-readiness synthesis} (\cref{sec:synthesis}) that scores
        each family on performance, fidelity, and threat-model fit, showing that
        real-world readiness comes from balancing the three rather than maximizing any
        one --- and locating each family on that frontier.
  \item \textbf{A stage\,$\times$\,mechanism gap-map} (\cref{sec:synthesis})
        whose empty cells are the field's open problems.
  \item \textbf{A focused vignette} (\cref{sec:obfuscation}): static
        obfuscation's reported resistance does not hold under the realistic
        threat model. We report the \emph{finding}; the attack methodology and a
        defense are deferred to a companion paper.
\end{itemize}
