\section{Hybrid Split: TEE + Obfuscation}
\label{sec:hybrid}

% confidential GPU (except GELO, which anchors in one). Sits on a performance-vs-threat
% spectrum BELOW confidential-GPU inference and static obfuscation on raw speed (it pays
% the TEE<->GPU serialization cost), but is more secure than static obfuscation and needs

\dnote{lead with the commodity-GPU argument; defer the deployment ranking to synthesis}

% --- scope-framing ---
The hybrid split keeps a small trusted core in a CPU-TEE (Intel TDX or AMD SEV-SNP) and
offloads the heavy linear algebra to an \emph{untrusted commodity GPU} over obfuscated
activations, recovering the exact result in the enclave. On a performance-versus-threat
spectrum it sits \emph{below} both pure confidential-GPU inference (\cref{sec:tee}) and
static obfuscation (\cref{sec:obfuscation}) on raw speed---it pays a real cost for the
TEE$\leftrightarrow$GPU communication it induces (\cref{sec:synthesis}), and some members
trade a little accuracy to offload more---but it buys two things in return: stronger
security than static obfuscation (the hiding randomness is refreshed, not fixed), and no
dependence on the scarce, expensive confidential-GPU hardware that pure-TEE inference
needs at scale. That trade---more security and commodity hardware, at a performance cost
relative to a confidential GPU---is the family's reason to exist; we substantiate where it
lands among the families in \cref{sec:synthesis}, not here.

\subsection{Approaches}
% Keep a small trusted core in a TEE; offload heavy linear algebra to an untrusted
% accelerator over OBFUSCATED activations. Map onto §2: TCB = TEE (small); HbC + white-box;
% observation surface = accelerator activations.
Every scheme in this family makes the same two-part design choice: \emph{what} to keep in
the enclave, and \emph{how} to hide the rest. A small trusted core---a CPU-TEE, or in
GELO's case a confidential GPU---holds the secrets and runs the operations that are cheap
but revealing: a secret transform and its inverse, the non-linearities, or merely the
obfuscation keys. The bulk of the cost, the dense linear algebra of the QKV and output
projections and the feed-forward GEMMs, is offloaded to an \emph{untrusted, commodity}
accelerator that never sees plaintext; correctness is recovered exactly in the enclave, so
unlike DP (\cref{sec:dp}) the family pays no fidelity tax. In the \cref{sec:framework}
taxonomy the TCB is the enclave alone, the adversary is an honest-but-curious, white-box
operator, and the observation surface is the stream of obfuscated activations crossing the
PCIe boundary.

The schemes differ in those two knobs. \textbf{GELO}~\citep{belikov_gelo} draws a
\emph{fresh} secret invertible matrix $A$ for each offloaded batch (a block of token
hidden states $H$), forms the mixed $U{=}AH$, offloads the Q/K/V and output projections on
$U$ to the untrusted GPU, and unmixes by $A^{-1}$ in the enclave; because $A$ is never
reused, recovering $H$ is a single-batch blind-source-separation problem. (GELO is the
family's outlier on hardware: its trusted core is a \emph{confidential} GPU---an
H200-class TEE---and it offloads to a pool of cheaper untrusted commodity GPUs such as
L40S, a cluster-economics choice that maximizes throughput when confidential GPUs are
scarce.) \textbf{TwinShield}~\citep{xue_twinshield} secret-shares \emph{both} the linear
and the non-linear layers across a CPU-TEE root and the accelerator---its OutAttnMult
recasts the data$\times$data attention multiply as additive computation with precomputed
offline terms, and OutSoftMax offloads the exponentials---and adds U-Verify integrity
checks (a Freivalds generalization, the first to verify the non-linear SoftMax) so the
accelerator may even be \emph{malicious}. \textbf{ObfuscaTune}~\citep{frikha_obfuscatune}
keeps only the low-parameter, non-linearity-bearing layers in the TEE---the input
embedding, the output head, and the LayerNorm/dropout layers, ${\sim}5\%$ of the
parameters---and offloads the obfuscated attention and MLP weights (the other $95\%$); its
obfuscation matrices are \emph{static}, set up once and reused across many inferences
(refreshed only periodically), a choice that proves fatal (below).
\textbf{SCX}~\citep{zeng_scx} protects the KV-cache not by encryption but by
\emph{permutation}---token- and feature-level permutations plus redundant embeddings and
calibrated Laplace noise (yielding $(\varepsilon,0)$-DP)---with one-time keys per decoding
step, so the cloud can neither recover the input nor complete the next token without the
user's key. \textbf{CMIF}~\citep{cmif} is a TEE\,+\,DP hybrid: it shields only the
embedding layer in a client-side CPU-TEE and, rather than encrypting activations (which
would force every non-linearity back into the enclave and pay the round-trip per layer),
it sanitizes the \emph{input tokens} with an optimized Report-Noisy-Max mechanism
($\varepsilon$-DP token substitution), so the sanitized sequence runs entirely on the
untrusted GPU with no per-layer return.

The reported numbers (\cref{tab:inf-perf}) trace a single curve: the more linear algebra a
scheme can safely offload, the closer it runs to plaintext---but the published figures are
\emph{not} measured on a common footing, and two of them need care.
GELO's headline ``${\approx}76\%$ offloaded at $20$--$30\%$ overhead'' is a
\emph{synthetic microbenchmark} of a \emph{single} projection offload---random tensors
shuttled between two processes, not an end-to-end model run---and that overhead is
dominated ($\sim$$80\%$) by inter-process communication, not the masking. Worse, the cost
is U-shaped in batch size: ${\sim}20\%$ only at a sweet-spot batch, rising again for large
batches as the $O(n^3)$ generation of the mixing matrix dominates; GELO also offloads only
the attention Q/K/V/O projections (MLP offload is left to future work). The number should
thus be read as a best-case lower bound, not a full-inference cost.
TwinShield offloads ${\sim}87\%$ of total transformer-inference \emph{FLOPs}---a compute
ratio, not a latency one---reporting $5.4\times$ over a TEE-only baseline in its verifiable
configuration (including U-Verify), ${\sim}6.7\times$ with privacy only, and
$4.0$--$6.1\times$\,/\,$4.9$--$7.7\times$ over prior verifiable\,/\,private schemes. But
offloading the non-linears is precisely what \emph{costs} crossings: the masks do not
commute with SoftMax, so TwinShield splits attention into \emph{per-head} OutAttnMult and
OutSoftMax calls and pays $(2H{+}5)L$ TEE$\leftrightarrow$GPU round-trips per forward pass
(\cref{tab:roundtrips})---the most of any scheme here---leaving its wall-clock latency
governed by serialization, not by the $13\%$ of FLOPs it retains. ObfuscaTune reports $1.5$--$4.3\times$ over unprotected
inference, and SCX is effectively free online ($\sim$$1\times$, $36$\,ms for Llama-7B)
having moved its key setup offline. Fidelity is exact for GELO/TwinShield/SCX and
near-exact for ObfuscaTune (numerical error grows with the obfuscation matrix's condition
number).

The binding constraint on this family is not the FLOP-offload ratio but the
TEE$\leftrightarrow$GPU \emph{serialization} it induces: each offloaded GEMM stalls until
the enclave returns the preceding non-linearity, idling the GPU on the PCIe round-trip.
\Cref{tab:roundtrips} makes this structural---it is the offload \emph{granularity}, not the
FLOP fraction, that sets latency, and the schemes span two orders of magnitude. TwinShield's
per-head offload is $O(HL)$ round-trips per token; GELO and ObfuscaTune are $O(L)$ (few
crossings, but a TEE-heavy residual---GELO keeps the FFN and all non-linears in the enclave);
SCX collapses decode to $O(1)$ per token by sharing intermediate-layer keys with the cloud
($O(L{+}T)$ overall); and CMIF reaches a single handoff by sanitizing the input so the whole
model runs GPU-side. The family's advantage is therefore economic---commodity untrusted GPUs,
where pure-TEE inference needs a confidential accelerator (\cref{sec:tee}) and cryptography
pays $100$--$1000\times$ (\cref{sec:crypto}); we rank it against the other families on the
deployment-readiness axes in \cref{sec:synthesis}.


\subsection{Limitations and Known Attacks}
% GELO Gram-leak (U^T U = H^T H under orthogonal mixing), mitigated by non-orthogonal A or
% shield vectors, per-batch only. Static-key schemes broken (Game of Arrows, static-basis).
The family's security rests on \emph{dynamic, never-reused} hiding randomness; where that
fails, so does the scheme (\cref{tab:hybrid-attacks}). The cautionary case is the
\emph{static} key: ObfuscaTune's fixed obfuscation matrices are recovered by Game of
Arrows~\citep{arrowmatch}, which exploits direction-similarity across the offloaded
weights to recover $>$$98\%$ of them, and the same static-basis weakness yields a full
confidentiality-and-integrity break (recovering a LLaMA-3-8B layer in
${\sim}6$~minutes)~\citep{saini_precomp_break}. The schemes that survive---GELO's
per-batch mixing, SCX's per-step keys---do so precisely because they refresh.

Even refreshed obfuscation leaks \emph{second-order} structure. Under an orthogonal mixing
$A$ the Gram matrix is invariant ($U^{\top}U{=}H^{\top}H$), exposing the pairwise inner
products of the hidden states. A non-orthogonal $A$, or appended high-energy ``shield''
vectors, blunt the resulting ICA/BSS and anchor attacks, but the guarantee is per-batch and
collapses under key reuse~\citep{belikov_gelo}. Finally, the externalized KV-cache that these schemes
leave outside the enclave for throughput is itself a reconstruction
surface---inversion, collision, and injection attacks rebuild the prompt from a leaked
cache~\citep{luo_shadowcache}, a threat made concrete by practical GPU-memory leaks such as
LeftoverLocals~\citep{leftoverlocals}---and the co-located TEE+GPU design pays for encrypting
the PCIe stream, with residual exposure from unencrypted HBM on some confidential-GPU parts.
Trust is honest-but-curious and white-box by default; only TwinShield's U-Verify extends to
a malicious accelerator.
