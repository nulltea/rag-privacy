Outsourcing Transformer inference and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to an
untrusted cloud exposes prompts, documents, and proprietary weights to the service
provider. A decade of point solutions now spans five mechanism families---secure
computation (MPC/FHE), trusted execution environments (TEEs), static obfuscation,
differential privacy, and hybrid TEE\,+\,obfuscation splits---but no systematization
unifies \emph{mechanism}, \emph{threat model}, and \emph{performance}; the closest
prior SoK is risk-centric, scoped to naive RAG, and omits a performance axis. We
systematize confidential Transformer inference and RAG on \emph{commodity hardware}
(no confidential GPU) along three axes: a six-dimension threat-model taxonomy, a
stage\,$\times$\,mechanism coverage map whose empty cells are the open problems, and a
performance comparison synthesized from reported numbers with an explicit
non-comparability caveat. The systematization spans inference, RAG, and graph-RAG---a
setting prior work excludes---and surfaces field-wide findings: the near-universal
exclusion of microarchitectural side-channels from threat models, the
under-reported invertibility of static obfuscation
(\needswork{headline recovery figure, vignette}), and a graph-RAG frontier in which
construction and traversal stages are barely covered by any confidentiality mechanism.
We position the hybrid split as the deployable frontier and map what remains open.
