Outsourcing Transformer inference and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to an
untrusted cloud exposes prompts, documents, and proprietary weights to the service
provider. A decade of point solutions now spans five mechanism families---secure
computation (MPC/FHE), trusted execution environments (TEEs), static obfuscation,
differential privacy, and hybrid TEE\,+\,obfuscation splits---but no systematization
unifies \emph{mechanism}, \emph{threat model}, and \emph{deployment cost}; the closest
prior SoK is risk-centric, scoped to naive RAG, and omits a performance axis. We
organize the field by \emph{deployment readiness}---the likelihood a scheme is adopted
in practice---assessed on three criteria: \emph{performance} (overhead and latency),
\emph{fidelity} (impact on output quality), and \emph{threat-model fit} (an adversary
model \emph{appropriate} to the deployment rather than maximal). A hardware-trust axis
of three tiers (no trusted hardware; a commodity CPU-TEE; a confidential accelerator)
feeds this assessment; confidential-accelerator deployments are treated as a
performance baseline and deferred to work that systematizes that hardware. Our
artifacts are a five-axis threat-model taxonomy, a stage\,$\times$\,mechanism
coverage map whose empty cells are open problems, a performance comparison from
reported numbers (with a non-comparability caveat), and a deployment-readiness
synthesis across families---spanning inference, RAG, and graph-RAG, a setting prior
work excludes. Findings include the near-universal exclusion of microarchitectural
side-channels from threat models, the under-reported invertibility of static
obfuscation (\needswork{headline recovery figure, validation case study}), nearly empty graph-RAG
coverage, and that deployment readiness comes not from maximizing any single axis but
from balancing all three---hybrid splits and obfuscation lead on the
performance-to-threat ratio, while exact-but-slow cryptography and hardware-gated TEEs
trail.
