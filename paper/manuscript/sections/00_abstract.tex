Outsourcing Transformer inference and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to an untrusted
cloud exposes prompts, documents, and proprietary weights to the provider. A decade of point
solutions now spans five mechanism families---secure computation (MPC/FHE), trusted execution
environments (TEEs), static obfuscation, differential privacy, and hybrid TEE\,+\,obfuscation
splits---but no systematization unifies \emph{mechanism}, \emph{threat model}, and
\emph{deployment cost} across them, and none covers the RAG retrieval layer. We organize the
field by \emph{deployment readiness}---the likelihood a scheme is adopted in practice---on three
criteria: \emph{performance}, \emph{fidelity} (impact on output quality), and \emph{threat-model
fit} (an adversary model \emph{appropriate} to the deployment, not maximal). Our artifacts are a
five-axis threat-model taxonomy, a stage\,$\times$\,mechanism coverage map whose empty cells are
open problems, a performance comparison normalized from reported numbers, and a
deployment-readiness synthesis---spanning inference, RAG, and graph-RAG, a setting prior work
excludes. We find that readiness comes not from maximizing any single axis but from balancing
all three: hybrid splits and obfuscation lead on the performance-to-threat ratio, while
exact-but-slow cryptography and hardware-gated TEEs trail. Two cross-cutting findings stand out:
the near-universal exclusion of microarchitectural side-channels from threat models, and the
under-reported invertibility of static obfuscation.
