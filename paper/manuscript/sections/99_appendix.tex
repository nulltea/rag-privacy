\section{Attack Validation of Deployment-Ready Schemes}
\label{app:validation}
% Decoupled from §5 and generalized from AloePri-only to the heuristic-basis deployment-ready
% set. FINDING + reproduction protocol only; the novel attack method (ArrowMatch/Sequence-IMA)
% and the defense are claimed in the companion paper, not here.
The deployment-readiness lens (\cref{sec:intro}) favors the schemes with the best
performance-to-threat ratio, and these are overwhelmingly \emph{heuristic-basis} schemes---static
obfuscation and obfuscation-hybrids---whose security is shown experimentally rather than proven.
For exactly those schemes, \emph{reported} resistance can exceed \emph{real} resistance: the
obfuscation is fixed and white-box, so an adversary can analyze it in advance and mount attacks
the original evaluation did not run. We therefore validate the most deployment-ready heuristic
schemes against a common attacker, as a data point for the gap-map (\cref{sec:synthesis}).
Cryptographic and pure-TEE schemes are excluded by construction: their guarantees are provable or
hardware-rooted, so there is no reported-vs-real gap to validate.

\paragraph{Reproduction protocol.} The adversary is a single, deliberately modest point in the
security space (\cref{sec:threat}): an honest-but-curious cloud operator, white-box on the public
model and the obfuscation method, denied only the ephemeral per-session secret, observing the
obfuscated service I/O, with side channels out of scope and a constrained, realistic budget
(\cref{sec:threat-budget}); its goal is to recover the input prompt. Each scheme is attacked
\emph{within its own reported regime}, with the attack family matched to its leakage surface
(\cref{tab:validation}); a break under these conditions is a break by the victim's own standard,
not an artefact of an inflated adversary.

\paragraph{Finding.} Across the set, reported resistance overstates real protection: the
obfuscation surface each scheme treats as its defence is precisely what an in-regime attack
targets, and several published inversions already recover substantially more plaintext than the
original evaluations report~\citep{lin_inversion,hnm,arrowmatch,tsqp,depth_false_privacy}. The
novel attack methodology (ArrowMatch / Sequence-IMA) and a defense that closes the gap are the
subject of the companion paper; here we report only the finding and the reproduction protocol,
as evidence for the deployment-readiness assessment of \cref{sec:synthesis}.
