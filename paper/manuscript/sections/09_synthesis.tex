The performance numbers above are one of three criteria that together determine whether
a scheme is likely to be \emph{deployed}: \cref{sec:intro}'s \emph{performance},
\emph{fidelity} (impact on output quality), and \emph{threat-model fit} (an adversary
model appropriate to the deployment, not maximal). \cref{tab:deployment} scores the
families on all three. The synthesis is deliberately not a ranking of families by
security: it shows that deployment readiness comes from \emph{balancing} the three axes,
not maximizing one. This family-level profile is the complement of the per-use-case
comparison (\cref{tab:performance}): the profiles say what each family is \emph{good at},
while the use-case view says which family is the deployable choice \emph{for a given

\paragraph{Finding: the client-reliance asymmetry.}
A fourth factor cuts across the three and is easy to miss because the trust ladder hides
it: \emph{how much work the scheme leaves on the client}. The lowest-server-trust
schemes---Tier-1 cryptographic retrieval with no trusted hardware---are precisely the ones
that make the client \emph{thick}. Compass and Pacmann achieve oblivious retrieval by
running the ORAM controller or the graph traversal \emph{client-side} (the client stores
the access state and drives a multi-round loop), and 2PC inference such as PermLLM makes
the user a compute party outright. ``Trust anchor: none'' reads as zero cost, but the cost
has only moved: from a trusted third party to the user's own device, which must now be
capable, stateful, and online for the duration of a query. For deployment, adoption, and
user experience this is a first-order constraint---a phone or a browser tab is a poor ORAM
controller---and it inverts the naive reading of the table: the families that look cheapest
on server trust can be the most demanding to actually field. A full per-scheme
classification (thin/stateful/thick) across all families is the natural next step;
\cref{tab:deployment} fills it for cryptography and flags the rest (\tq).

\paragraph{Cross-comparison: a frontier, not a trajectory.}
Andreoletti et al.~\citep{andreoletti_ppi_practice} survey an overlapping set of families through
the same deployability lens and conclude with a single trust-minimising \emph{trajectory}---TEE as
today's pragmatic baseline, crypto-augmentation as a mid-term step, and FHE as the asymptotic
endpoint. Our scheme-level scoring shares their starting point (Tier-3 TEE is the only near-plaintext
option for full-scale models today) but not the linear ordering: the hybrid split and, on cost alone,
static obfuscation already sit at the readiness frontier (\cref{tab:deployment})---they are
present-day operating points, not way-stations toward FHE, whose cost keeps it off the frontier for
generative workloads. One factor a family-level view understates is \emph{infrastructural}: because
obfuscation and DP leave the model graph unmodified, they drop into an existing production serving
stack (vLLM, llama.cpp) with no bespoke runtime, whereas MPC, FHE, and TEE each demand a dedicated
serving path. The deployable choice is thus \emph{use-case dependent} (\cref{tab:performance})---a
frontier across which different families win different stages, not a single endpoint.
Obfuscation's place on that frontier is, however, conditional on a design discipline that
the attack record makes explicit (\cref{sec:obfusc-attacks},~\cref{sec:hybrid}): the schemes
that survive refresh their hiding randomness per query or per batch, while every broken
scheme reuses a static permutation or key that a white-box adversary averages across queries
to recover.

\subsection{Graph-RAG as a Retrieval Setting}
Graph-RAG systems such as LightRAG~\citep{guo_lightrag} index a corpus as a knowledge
graph; their retrieval surface is entity/relation ANN plus \emph{one-hop} neighborhood
expansion and a source-chunk fan-out---not multi-hop reasoning or GNN message-passing. The
schemes that cover this surface are catalogued with the other retrieval work in
\cref{sec:retrieval} (\cref{ssec:retr-graph}); here we record only the cross-family finding.
Graph-RAG is the most privacy-underserved setting in the corpus: Bodea et
al.~\citep{bodea_sok} exclude it entirely, secure GNN-inference and graph-analytics schemes
are off-target for the one-hop surface (cited only as lineage), and the near-empty graph rows
of \cref{tab:gapmap} are the resulting open frontier---most acute at the \emph{construction}
stage and for traversal integrity (ZKGraph~\citep{zkgraph} is the lone entry). The
distinctive adjacency/degree leak that separates graph-RAG from naive RAG is the volume
surface that volume-hiding multi-maps target (\cref{ssec:retr-graph}).

\subsection{Finding: the Field-Wide Side-Channel Blind Spot}
\label{ssec:sidechannel}

Reading down the residual-leakage axis of the taxonomy (\cref{tab:threat-taxonomy})
yields a uniform answer: \emph{every} scheme places microarchitectural, cache, timing,
power, and physical channels out of scope. GELO excludes ``side channels
originating inside the TEE \dots\ and accelerator-side leakage beyond raw memory
reads'' \citep{belikov_gelo}; Opal devotes a paragraph to enumerating the
in-enclave side channels it does \emph{not} defend \citep{kaviani_opal}; the
remainder simply omit them. This is a collective blind spot worth naming,
because it falls hardest on the schemes least able to absorb it: the TEE and
hybrid designs anchor their entire trust in hardware whose best-documented
weakness is exactly microarchitectural and transient-execution leakage---the
Spectre/Foreshadow-class attacks \needswork{cite Spectre, Foreshadow, PLATYPUS,
and the TEE side-channel surveys}. A scheme that trusts a TEE while declaring
side channels out of scope is assuming away the dominant historical failure
mode of that very anchor.

We do not close this gap: side channels are out of scope for this SoK as
well, and current confidential-computing hardware
(TDX-backed CVMs, GPUs in confidential mode) materially raises the bar against
them \citep{kaviani_opal}. But we record it as a systematisation finding and a
standing item for future work: the field has converged on a threat model that
structurally excludes the attack class most likely to break its dominant trust
anchor.
