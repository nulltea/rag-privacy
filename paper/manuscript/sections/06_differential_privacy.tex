\section{Differential Privacy}
\label{sec:dp}

Differential privacy (DP) buys a formal but probabilistic guarantee by adding calibrated
noise. Several schemes in other families borrow DP machinery: the covariant obfuscation of
\cref{sec:obfuscation} adds Gaussian noise to weights and analyzes it under a Rényi-metric
relaxation, ObfusLM perturbs synthesized embedding weights under a Laplace mechanism, and the
hybrid SCX of \cref{sec:hybrid} injects Laplace noise into its KV-cache encoding. What sets the
schemes in this section apart is not that they use noise but where the guarantee comes from. In
the obfuscation and hybrid cases the noise is an adjunct to a secret key (a permutation, a
one-time encoding) and is applied \emph{statically to the model's weights}; protection ultimately
rests on the secret, and if it leaks the noise alone does not save it.

The schemes here derive
protection from the \emph{randomness alone}, with no secret to keep: the bound holds against an
adversary who knows the weights, the mechanism, and the budget, and the noise is drawn
\emph{fresh per request} and applied to the data or the output rather than baked into the
weights. The price is paid purely in fidelity, which degrades as the budget $\varepsilon$ shrinks.
A second axis decides what the family can protect, and we use it to organize the approaches:
whether the noise is added \emph{locally} (the client perturbs its own input) or \emph{centrally}
(a trusted operator perturbs an output), which fixes the protected asset and the adversary, the
confidentiality-scope axis of \cref{sec:threat-axes}. On the deployment-readiness criteria DP
scores well on performance, near-plaintext at inference, and offers a formal threat model,
trading fidelity as $\varepsilon$ shrinks (\cref{sec:synthesis}).

\subsection{Approaches}
The schemes agree on the primitive, calibrated noise under a budget $\varepsilon$, but differ on
\emph{which} DP notion they target and \emph{where} the noise enters. The noise mechanism fixes
the notion: the Gaussian mechanism yields $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP with tight Rényi composition;
the Laplace mechanism and its generalized (metric) form yield pure $\varepsilon$-DP and
metric-LDP, whose indistinguishability degrades with the distance between inputs; and the
exponential mechanism handles discrete selection, where additive noise is meaningless, by sampling
an output with probability tilted toward high utility. Where the noise enters splits the family
into a local branch (input and embedding) and a central branch (output).

\paragraph{Local DP: input and embedding.}
In the local model the client perturbs its own data before sending it, so the operator is never
trusted; the cost is that the client must run the encoder, moving part of the compute and trust
base onto the device. Token-level local DP replaces discrete tokens through an exponential
mechanism (SANTEXT, InferDPT); as a fixed client-side map it is one of the static input transforms
of \cref{sec:obfuscation}, and we treat it there. The three embedding schemes (\cref{tab:inf-perf})
form a progression in \emph{how much of the noise is learned}. \textbf{DP-Forward}~\cite{du_dpforward}
learns nothing about the noise: it adds an analytic matrix-Gaussian calibrated only to the $L_2$
sensitivity of the pre-noise layers, a \emph{task-agnostic} mechanism (the noise does not depend on
the downstream task), and the model is fine-tuned to perform on the noisy embeddings; the guarantee
is standard $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP, in its local sequence form $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-SeqLDP
(${\approx}94\%$ on SST-2). \textbf{SPARSE}~\cite{sparse_dp} learns \emph{where} the noise goes: a
concept mask scores which embedding dimensions carry a user-named sensitive attribute, and a
Mahalanobis (elliptical Laplace) mechanism perturbs those dimensions more than the rest, under
metric-LDP (keeping ${\approx}65\%$ utility at $\varepsilon{=}5$ while cutting leakage from
$60\%$ to $19\%$). \textbf{NVDP}~\cite{nvdp} learns \emph{the noise distribution itself}: a
nonparametric variational information-bottleneck layer trains a posterior (a learned distribution)
over the multi-vector embedding and samples the shared embedding from it, calibrating the noise to
the task and reporting a Rényi-divergence bound that converts to a Bayesian-DP guarantee
($83.0\%$ on MRPC at a Bayesian-DP budget $\varepsilon_\mu{\approx}11$). The three are genuinely
distinct: they differ in DP notion (standard $(\varepsilon,\delta)$, metric, Bayesian-Rényi) and in
what is learned (nothing, which dimensions, the whole distribution), so none subsumes another.
Because any function of a noised embedding inherits its bound by the post-processing property of
DP, these local schemes already protect the user's own input through to the output.

\paragraph{Central DP: output.}
The output schemes invert the trust setup. Here a \emph{trusted} operator holds the private data
and injects the noise server-side; the client adds nothing. They protect not the user's own input
but third-party data the operator holds, against the party that \emph{consumes} the output, so
their confidentiality scope (\cref{sec:threat-axes}) differs from the local schemes above. Both act
at the generation stage. \textbf{DP-KSA}~\cite{tang_dprag} (the inference half of DP-RAG) treats
the retrieval corpus as the private dataset: it draws an ensemble of candidate generations and
releases a differentially private set of answer keywords through the propose-test-release framework
with an exponential-mechanism count, so the answer is $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP with respect to
whether any one document is in the corpus. Its retrieval is ordinary dense top-$k$; the privacy is
on the generated answer, not the query or the access pattern, which is why it sits here and not
with the retrieval schemes of \cref{sec:retrieval}. \textbf{DP-Fusion}~\cite{dp_fusion} bounds the
influence of sensitive spans already inside the model's context: at each decode step it runs the
model once on a public, sensitive-token-free version of the context and once per sensitive group,
blends each private next-token distribution toward the public one until their Rényi divergence
falls under the per-group budget, and samples from the mixture. The operator runs the model and
sees the context in the clear, with no trusted hardware; what is bounded is how much the marked
spans can move the generated output, so the guarantee defends whoever \emph{receives} that output
and, applied per retrieved chunk, doubles as a prompt-injection defense. The two are complementary
rather than interchangeable: DP-KSA protects corpus membership, DP-Fusion protects context spans,
and central output-DP is the natural complement to the local schemes when the protected asset is
the operator's data rather than the user's input.

\subsection{Performance and Accuracy}
At inference every scheme here is near-plaintext in compute: the local schemes add one noise draw
to the forward pass, the central schemes add sampling over the output. The real costs sit elsewhere
and split along the local/central line. The local schemes pay \emph{offline}, once: DP-Forward
fine-tunes under the noise layer at roughly the cost of an ordinary fine-tune (and about
$3\times$ cheaper than the DP-SGD it replaces); SPARSE learns its concept mask in a one-time pass
that scales linearly with data (25\,min for 10k samples, under 45\,min for 20k, on a single
RTX~3090); NVDP trains one extra information-bottleneck layer over a frozen encoder. Once trained,
all three serve at ${\approx}1\times$, and because post-processing is free under DP the noised
embedding can be reused across tasks. The central schemes pay \emph{online}: DP-KSA multiplies
generation by its ensemble size (${\approx}N\times$ calls, with $N{\approx}80$), and DP-Fusion runs
$m{+}1$ forward passes per token for $m$ sensitive groups. DP-Fusion's passes parallelize, so
wall-clock can stay near a single pass, but only when compute and memory are spare; the
$m{+}1\times$ in compute and memory is real, and a multi-group deployment needs an inference path
that issues and fuses the parallel passes (the method ships as a wrapper over standard models, so
it does not demand a bespoke engine, but it does require that orchestration).

How privacy trades against cost differs sharply from static obfuscation, where security, accuracy,
and speed move together. For the local schemes $\varepsilon$ is a pure \emph{accuracy} knob: a
tighter budget costs fidelity, not compute, since the noise draw is the same size at any
$\varepsilon$, and a more selective mechanism (SPARSE's shaped noise, NVDP's learned posterior)
costs only offline training. For the central schemes stronger privacy does cost compute, because it
is bought with more samples: a larger ensemble for DP-KSA, a higher $m$ for DP-Fusion. Fidelity is
the cost the whole family ultimately pays, read as task-accuracy or recall loss, small in the
loose-$\varepsilon$ regime each paper reports and severe as $\varepsilon$ approaches $0$, where the
output becomes independent of the protected data. The rate in between is not universal: it depends
on the mechanism and on the content protected, and even within DP-Fusion the divergence grows
differently for different entity types (roughly logarithmic for code-like spans, closer to a power
law for dates), so no single sub- or super-linear law describes the frontier.

The attacks on this family fall into two classes by whether DP can answer them
(\cref{tab:dp-attacks}). Some are \emph{mitigable} within the mechanism. The local embedding
schemes are all targets of embedding inversion: Vec2Text and GEIA reconstruct text from
representations, and DP-Forward in particular is recovered by a trained inversion model
(IMA, over $75\%$ token recovery in AloePri's evaluation) once $\varepsilon$ is loosened enough to
keep utility. Tightening $\varepsilon$ or shaping the noise pushes these back (SPARSE's
concept-masked noise cuts Vec2Text leakage from $60\%$ to $19\%$ at $\varepsilon{=}10$), so the
inversion threat is managed on the $\varepsilon$-utility frontier rather than removed. DP-Fusion's
gap is coverage, not mechanism: its bound holds only over spans a tagger marks sensitive, so a
missed span falls outside the guarantee, and a higher-recall named-entity recognizer closes it.

The remaining limits are \emph{structural} and cannot be repaired with more DP. Two of the notions
traded here are weaker than standard $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP and should be read as such:
metric-LDP (SPARSE) only makes inputs indistinguishable in proportion to their distance in
embedding space, the $\ell_2$ metric on the embedding vector rather than a ranking score, so
far-apart inputs stay distinguishable and nearest-neighbour search still separates them; and
Bayesian-DP (NVDP) measures the shift in an adversary's belief against an \emph{assumed} data
distribution rather than the worst case, so its budget is only as honest as that assumption, and a
distributional mismatch, or a GAN-style reconstruction trained on the true distribution, erodes it.
The $\varepsilon$-utility frontier itself is structural: at $\varepsilon\to 0$ the output is
independent of the protected data, so there is a floor below which no scheme in this family can
both protect and function. And composition across pipeline stages is unresolved: a deployment that
perturbs embeddings and then bounds the output spends two budgets whose joint guarantee is not
simply additive, an open problem Bodea et al.\ also flag for RAG. The payoff that survives these
caveats is structural too: central output-DP composes orthogonally with any retrieval or inference
backend, which is what makes it the family's natural answer to the corpus-extraction attacks of
\cref{sec:synthesis}, regardless of how the rest of the pipeline is protected.
