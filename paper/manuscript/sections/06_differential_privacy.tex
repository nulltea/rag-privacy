\section{Differential Privacy}
\label{sec:dp}

Differential privacy (DP) provides a formal but probabilistic guarantee by adding calibrated
noise. Several schemes in other families borrow DP machinery: the covariant obfuscation of
\cref{sec:obfuscation} adds Gaussian noise to weights and analyzes it under Rényi-metric
DP, ObfusLM perturbs synthesized embedding weights under a Laplace mechanism, and the
hybrid SCX of \cref{sec:hybrid} injects Laplace noise into its KV-cache encoding. What sets the
schemes in this section apart is not that they use noise but where the guarantee comes from. In
the obfuscation and hybrid cases the noise is an adjunct to a secret key (a permutation, a
one-time encoding) and is applied statically to the model's weights; protection ultimately
rests on the secret, and if it leaks the noise alone does not protect the data.

The schemes here derive
protection from the \emph{randomness alone}, with no secret to keep: the bound holds against an
adversary who knows the weights, the mechanism, and the budget, and the noise is drawn
fresh per request and applied to the data or the output rather than embedded in the
weights. The only cost is accuracy: a smaller $\varepsilon$ injects more noise and lowers it.

Within the family, the approaches divide by \emph{whose} data they protect and from \emph{whom},
the confidentiality-scope axis of \cref{sec:threat-axes}: a scheme either protects the user's own
input from an untrusted operator, or protects the operator's retrieval corpus and context from the
consumer of the answer. These map onto the two standard settings of DP, local and central: the
first noises the input on the client, the second noises the output on a trusted server. On the deployment-readiness criteria DP
scores well on performance, near-plaintext at inference, and offers a formal threat model,
trading accuracy for that guarantee (\cref{sec:synthesis}).

\subsection{Approaches}
The schemes agree on the primitive, calibrated noise under a budget $\varepsilon$, but differ on
\emph{which} DP notion they target and \emph{where} the noise enters. The noise mechanism fixes
the notion. Additive mechanisms cover the continuous case: the Gaussian mechanism yields
$(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP with tight Rényi composition, while the Laplace mechanism and its
generalized (metric) form yield pure $\varepsilon$-DP and metric-LDP, whose indistinguishability
degrades with the Euclidean/Mahalanobis distance between inputs. The exponential mechanism instead
handles discrete selection, where additive noise is meaningless, by sampling an output with
probability that increases with utility.

\paragraph{Protecting the user's input (local DP).}
In local DP, the client perturbs its own data before sending it, so the operator is never
trusted; the cost is that the client must run the encoder, moving part of the computation, and the
trusted computing base, onto the device. Token-level local DP replaces discrete tokens through an exponential
mechanism (SANTEXT, InferDPT); as a fixed client-side map it is one of the static input transforms
of \cref{sec:obfuscation}, and we treat it there. The three embedding-level schemes form a progression in \emph{how much of the noise mechanism is
trained} rather than fixed by a closed-form formula; model retraining mirrors this inversely,
heaviest where the noise is fixed and minimal where the noise is trained.
\textbf{DP-Forward}~\cite{du_dpforward} trains none of the noise: it adds a fixed analytic
matrix-Gaussian calibrated only to the $L_2$ sensitivity of the pre-noise layers, a
\emph{task-agnostic} mechanism (the noise does not depend on the downstream task). It pays for that
fixed noise with the heaviest model training of the three, fine-tuning the whole pipeline to perform
on the noisy embeddings; the guarantee is standard $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP, in its local sequence
form $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-SeqLDP (${\approx}92\%$ on SST-2 at $\varepsilon{\approx}16$). \textbf{SPARSE}~\cite{sparse_dp}
trains \emph{where} the noise goes: over a frozen encoder, a concept mask scores which embedding
dimensions carry a user-specified sensitive attribute, and a Mahalanobis (elliptical Laplace) mechanism
perturbs those dimensions more than the rest, under metric-LDP (keeping ${\approx}65\%$ utility at
$\varepsilon{=}10$ while cutting leakage from $60\%$ to $19\%$). \textbf{NVDP}~\cite{nvdp} trains
\emph{the noise distribution itself}. It adds a single nonparametric variational
information-bottleneck (NVIB) layer on a pretrained encoder, which fits a posterior over the
multi-vector embedding and samples the shared embedding from it. The noise is thus calibrated to the
task, and its Rényi-divergence bound converts to a Bayesian-DP guarantee (${\approx}83\%$ on MRPC at
$\varepsilon_\mu{\approx}11$).

Each scheme thus occupies a different design point along these two axes, the DP notion and the
trained part of the mechanism, with minimal model retraining except for DP-Forward, which absorbs
its fixed noise wholesale.
\emph{The output is not hidden and gains no privacy of its own:} the operator returns it in the clear, and
the noise costs it only accuracy. The perturbation protects the input, keeping the operator
from inverting either the embedding or the output back to it.

\paragraph{Protecting the operator's context (central DP).}
The output schemes invert the trust assumptions. Here a \emph{trusted} operator holds the private data
and injects the noise server-side; the client adds nothing. They protect not the user's own input
but third-party data the operator holds, against the party that \emph{consumes} the output, so
their confidentiality scope (\cref{sec:threat-axes}) differs from that of the local schemes above. \textbf{DP-KSA}~\cite{tang_dprag} (the DP-RAG generation method) treats
the retrieval corpus as the private dataset: it draws an ensemble of candidate generations and
releases a differentially private set of answer keywords through the propose-test-release framework
with an exponential-mechanism count, so the answer is $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP with respect to
whether any one document is in the corpus. Its retrieval is ordinary dense top-$k$; the privacy is
on the generated answer, not the query or the access pattern. \textbf{DP-Fusion}~\cite{dp_fusion} bounds how much sensitive spans in the context can move the
output: at each decode step it blends the next-token distribution computed with those spans
toward one computed without them, until their Rényi divergence falls under the per-group
budget, then samples from the mixture. The operator runs the model and sees the context in the
clear, with no trusted hardware; the bound defends whoever \emph{receives} the output, and applied
per retrieved chunk it doubles as a prompt-injection defense. The two central schemes cover different operator-side assets: DP-KSA protects corpus membership,
while DP-Fusion protects context spans.

\begin{openproblem}
\label{op:dp-compose}
\emph{Composing local and central DP.} The two are orthogonal, protecting different assets on
opposite sides of the trust boundary, so in principle a single pipeline could apply both, hiding the
user's input from the operator and the operator's corpus and context from the consumer. Whether the
two noise sources then compose without compounding the accuracy loss is untested: no surveyed work
runs DP mitigations in sequence~\cite{bodea_sok}.
\end{openproblem}

\subsection{Performance and Accuracy}
At inference every scheme here is near-plaintext in compute: the local schemes add one noise draw
to the forward pass, the central schemes add sampling over the output. The real costs lie elsewhere
and split along the local/central line. The local schemes pay \emph{offline}, once: DP-Forward
fine-tunes under the noise layer at roughly the cost of an ordinary fine-tune; SPARSE trains its concept mask in a one-time pass
that scales linearly with data (25\,min for 10k samples, under 45\,min for 20k, on a single
RTX~3090); NVDP trains one extra information-bottleneck layer on a pretrained encoder. Once trained,
all three serve at ${\approx}1\times$, and because post-processing is free under DP the noised
embedding can be reused across tasks.

The central schemes pay \emph{online}: DP-KSA multiplies
generation by its ensemble size (${\approx}N\times$ calls, with $N{\approx}80$). DP-Fusion runs
$m{+}1$ forward passes per token for $m$ sensitive groups; because these passes parallelize,
wall-clock can stay near a single pass, but only when compute and memory are available. Deployment needs an inference path
that issues and fuses the parallel passes (the method is implemented as a wrapper around standard
models, so it does not require a custom inference engine, but it does require that orchestration).

Within this family the three axes are only loosely coupled: privacy and accuracy always trade
against each other through $\varepsilon$, whereas privacy and performance interact only on the
central side. For the local schemes, turning $\varepsilon$ down (more privacy) costs only accuracy, not compute,
since the noise draw is the same size at any $\varepsilon$, and a more selective mechanism (SPARSE's
masked noise, NVDP's learned posterior) costs only offline training. The central schemes trade the
same way; here the answer degrades toward its \emph{no-private-context} baseline (the answer the
model would give without the corpus or context at all) as $\varepsilon$ decreases: DP-KSA's accuracy
drops to the no-retrieval baseline near $\varepsilon{\approx}1$ and rises above it by
$\varepsilon{\approx}2$, while DP-Fusion's answer approaches the one it would give with the sensitive
spans removed.

How steeply accuracy falls follows from
each mechanism's $\varepsilon$-to-noise map: additive mechanisms (Gaussian, Laplace) scale noise as
${\propto}1/\varepsilon$, a convex curve that is flat at moderate $\varepsilon$ and collapses below
$\varepsilon{\approx}1$, with the collapse point set by the sensitivity of what is perturbed.
Selection and blending mechanisms (the exponential mechanism, distribution mixing) instead saturate,
rising with $\varepsilon$ above a release threshold and flattening once the budget is loose.

The attacks on this family fall into two classes by whether DP can answer them
(\cref{tab:dp-attacks}). Some are \emph{mitigable} within the mechanism. The local embedding
schemes are all targets of embedding inversion: Vec2Text~\cite{vec2text} and GEIA~\cite{geia}
reconstruct text from representations, and DP-Forward in particular is recovered by a trained inversion model
(IMA, over $75\%$ token recovery in AloePri's evaluation) once $\varepsilon$ is loosened enough to
keep utility. Lowering $\varepsilon$ or concentrating the noise on the sensitive dimensions weakens
these attacks (SPARSE's concept-masked noise cuts Vec2Text leakage from $60\%$ to $19\%$ at
$\varepsilon{=}10$), so the
inversion threat is managed on the $\varepsilon$-utility frontier rather than removed. DP-Fusion's
gap is coverage, not mechanism: its bound holds only over spans a tagger marks sensitive, so a
missed span falls outside the guarantee, and a higher-recall tagger closes it.

The remaining limits are \emph{structural} and cannot be repaired with more DP. Two of the notions
traded here are weaker than standard $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP. Metric-LDP (SPARSE) only makes two
inputs indistinguishable in proportion to their $\ell_2$ distance in embedding space, so far-apart
inputs can be separated with nearest-neighbour search. Bayesian-DP (NVDP)
bounds the shift in an adversary's belief against an \emph{assumed} data distribution, not the worst
case. Its privacy is therefore only as sound as that assumption: a distributional mismatch, or a
GAN-style reconstruction trained on the true distribution, weakens it.
The $\varepsilon$-utility frontier itself is structural: at $\varepsilon\to 0$ the output is
independent of the protected data, so there is a floor below which no scheme in this family can
both protect and function.

Stacking mitigations across pipeline stages is likewise unresolved: the
joint privacy guarantee and combined accuracy cost of running local and central DP together are both
uncharacterized (\cref{op:dp-compose}). One advantage holds despite all of these limits: central
output-DP composes with any retrieval or inference backend, which makes it the family's natural
answer to the corpus-extraction attacks of \cref{sec:synthesis}, regardless of how the rest of the
pipeline is protected.
