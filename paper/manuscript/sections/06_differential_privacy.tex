\section{Differential Privacy}
\label{sec:dp}

% --- scope-framing (short role-framing; bodies stay \needswork) ---
Differential privacy adds calibrated noise to obtain a formal---but
probabilistic---guarantee, applied to embeddings (a noised forward pass), to queries
(distance-DP perturbation), or to the generated output (output-DP against extraction).
A practical divide runs through the family: embedding-DP typically requires
\emph{retraining} or fine-tuning the model under the noise mechanism---a substantial
deployment cost---whereas query- and output-DP attach to an unmodified model.
Like obfuscation its inference cost is near-zero compute; unlike obfuscation it buys a provable
bound, paid for in utility as $\varepsilon$ tightens. The output-side variant
(DP-RAG/DP-KSA) is notable for composing orthogonally with any retrieval mechanism,
making it a generic defense against the corpus-extraction attacks of
\cref{sec:synthesis}. On the deployment-readiness criteria, DP scores
well on performance and offers a formal threat model, trading \emph{fidelity}
(recall/accuracy) as $\varepsilon$ tightens (\cref{sec:synthesis}).

\subsection{Representative Schemes}
The DP \emph{inference} schemes---DP-Forward (matrix-Gaussian noise in the embedding
forward pass), SPARSE (metric-DP embedding mask), and DP-KSA (output-DP on the generated
answer)---are reported in the shared inference table (\cref{tab:inf-perf}). The query-side DP
retrieval scheme RemoteRAG is catalogued with the retrieval work (\cref{sec:retrieval}).
RemoteRAG applies DistanceDP to the \emph{query} to coarse-retrieve a candidate set, then
computes exact top-$k$ similarity over those candidates under homomorphic encryption;
as a retrieval scheme it is covered in \cref{sec:retrieval} (\cref{tab:crypto-vec}).
