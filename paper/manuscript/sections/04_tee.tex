A trusted execution environment runs the computation in plaintext inside a
hardware-isolated enclave, attested to the client over RATLS. Pure-TEE inference is
our \emph{baseline}: it achieves near-plaintext speed and shifts trust to the silicon
vendor, but at full scale it demands either a CPU enclave large enough for the model
(Tier~2, slow for large models) or a \emph{confidential accelerator} (Tier~3---an
H100/B200-class GPU-TEE). The schemes here are therefore predominantly Tier~3, which we
keep as the performance/trust baseline rather than systematize in depth: once the
accelerator is trusted end-to-end, confidentiality reduces to enclave construction and
attestation, a hardware-specific space systematized separately~\citep{accel_tee_sok}.
That Tier-3 hardware requirement---not the performance---is what motivates the Tier-2
commodity-GPU hybrid split (\cref{sec:hybrid}). The strongest adversary in our corpus
appears here: Opal~\cite{kaviani_opal} assumes a \emph{malicious host} and additionally
hides access patterns with ORAM. On the deployment-readiness criteria, pure TEE scores
highest on performance and fidelity, but its Tier-3
hardware dependence is the binding deployability constraint (\cref{sec:synthesis}).

End-to-end confidentiality is not the enclave alone but a deployment workflow that binds the
hardware guarantee to the threat model~\citep{andreoletti_ppi_practice}: (i)~\emph{remote
attestation}---the client verifies the enclave's measurement (expected model, runtime, config);
(ii)~\emph{key provisioning}---session keys are released to the enclave only after attestation;
(iii)~\emph{secure channel}---prompts are decrypted and processed only inside the enclave, and
completions are re-encrypted before leaving it; (iv)~\emph{output handling}---plaintext logging,
tracing, and telemetry outside the enclave boundary are forbidden by policy. Steps (i)--(iii) are
where TEE schemes concentrate; step~(iv), together with token-streaming and metadata
(length/timing), is the residual-leakage surface the side-channel finding of \cref{sec:synthesis}
targets.

\subsection{Representative Schemes}
The pure-TEE inference baselines---H100~CC (whole-GPU enclave) and PipeLLM (speculative
pipelined PCIe encryption)---are reported in the shared inference table (\cref{tab:inf-perf}),
at $4$--$8\%$ and $<$19.6\% overhead respectively.
Opal is an end-to-end graph-RAG system and the corpus's strongest adversary (a
\emph{malicious host}, hiding access patterns via ORAM; cf.\ \cref{tab:threat-taxonomy}); we
keep it here as the TEE baseline and detail its retrieval role with the other RAG schemes in
\cref{sec:retrieval}. The TEE oblivious-RAM substrate H$_2$O$_2$RAM, being a
retrieval/storage primitive, is likewise covered there.
